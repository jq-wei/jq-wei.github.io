Coding agents as control designers: rendered attempts

112 tasks · for each, the best agent attempt and one attempt per failure mode, re-run on public seeds. Built by scripts/portal.py.

This page accompanies a paper on coding agents as control designers. Each task below asks an agent (Claude Code with Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1 or Sonnet 5.5; one session of at most an hour; no neural networks) to write a feedback controller for a simulated plant, and grades the file on held-out seeds. Every video re-runs the final controller of one session through the task's own evaluator on a public seed; the table under it is the official grade on the held-out seeds. For each task the best attempt is shown and one attempt per failure mode, and for a few solved tasks the weakest passing attempt as well. A few clips come from an earlier sweep with other models under a different harness; their pages say so. A few tasks were built after the paper's sweep and are not in its counts; their pages say so too. The tasks, the controllers with their grades and the scripts that build this page are released with the paper. A follow-up on session budget and effort (one hour at effort max, two hours at effort high, on six tasks) is summarised at the end of this page. The benchmark release adds eleven task families (first section below), evaluated with Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 at effort max; where a plant has no scene to film, the attempt is shown as an animated plot or a figure. Their results are in the leaderboard that follows.

Leaderboard: benchmark v1

Index by model, with 95 % intervals; tables written by scripts/index.py into docs/leaderboard/.

The benchmark release adds 55 tasks in 11 plant families to the tasks shown below. Their gates are pegged to a frozen incumbent (an expert controller, an LQG or H-infinity synthesis, a design with the true model, an RL expert policy, the best published value), to a physical specification or to a published standard. Protocol: Claude Code at effort max, one session of the task's time limit, at most three sessions per task and model, run one after the other with early stopping (after the first session if it passed within 20 agent minutes, after the second if both failed). A task's score is passed sessions over sessions run. The Index is the mean over the families of the mean score of their tasks, with a 95 % interval from a stratified bootstrap. A session that the sandbox killed or that the provider's safeguards stopped is void: counted, not scored. Cost is the sessions' own accounting.

Budget and effort: a follow-up on Fable's six failed tasks

One hour at effort max, and two hours at effort high, against the sweep's one hour at effort high.

In the sweep, Fable 5.1 passed none of its three sessions on six tasks where Opus 5.5 passed two or three: hopper-parity-notune, hopper-blackout, hopper-brisk-robust, humanoid-uneven, humanoid-forward-proprio, hopper-blackout-noisy. Two follow-up arms re-ran these six tasks for all three models, three sessions per task and model, changing one thing each: the effort setting (max instead of high, still one hour) or the clock (two hours instead of one, still effort high). Images, grader and held-out seeds are the sweep's. These sessions are not in the paper's main counts and not in the clips above. Passes out of 18 sessions:

  1. Effort max and a second hour buy Fable the same thing, and neither closes the gap. Each moves Fable from 0 to 8 or 9 passes in 18, within one pass of the other on every task. Opus moves too (12 to 15 and 16), so Fable's two-hour record still trails Opus's one-hour record on three tasks (parity-notune, uneven, blackout-noisy), equals it on two (blackout, brisk-robust) and beats it on one (forward-proprio). With three sessions per cell these are directions, not significant differences.
  2. The second hour rarely binds. Of the 32 two-hour passes, seven or eight were installed or first confirmed after minute 60, three of them Fable's, each a tuner refining a design already in place. Where Fable gained most (forward-proprio 0 to 3, blackout 0 to 2) no pass used the second hour: its one-hour sessions froze a design at minutes 37 to 48 to leave time for validation, its two-hour sessions kept iterating to minutes 40 to 55. The longer budget acts on how the first hour is spent.
  3. On the blackout pair the deciding variable is a design choice, not time. Every passing controller, from every model at every budget, keeps a private MuJoCo copy of the plant, resets it to the last fresh measurement and steps it with its own last action through the blackout, so the gait underneath sees a true state. Fable's pass count on these two tasks equals the number of its sessions that made that choice (0, 1, 2 on blackout and 0, 1, 1 on blackout-noisy at high, max, two hours); its failures propagated the state by hand or controlled on the stale measurement.
  4. More time can buy a controller the grader cannot time. Opus's two two-hour losses are sampling-based MPCs, about 45 to 55 s of compute per 15 s episode, that passed the agent's own checks and then exceeded the verifier's 900 s budget, a cost the instruction does not state.

Counting: a few sessions lost to the host machine (setup download failures, one host sleep) were re-run and the re-runs are counted; counting the three sleep-cut sessions instead gives Fable 8 and Sonnet 8 at two hours.
