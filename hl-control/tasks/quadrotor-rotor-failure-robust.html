quadrotor-rotor-failure-robust

Quadrotor losing a rotor (Skydio X2, MuJoCo) · solved 6 of 6 agent attempts pass

The task. Open when it was built: the best controller known then failed this gate.

more

Frontier tier of the quadrotor family. Quadrotor commanded at the level of its four rotor thrusts (Skydio X2 of MuJoCo Menagerie, 30 ms motor lag); gyroscope and accelerometer with bias and noise, position and yaw from a 50 Hz tracker 30 ms late, no velocity or attitude; mass (+-10 %), thrust coefficient (+-5 %) and centre of mass drawn per seed and hidden; 3-6 m/s wind with Dryden turbulence (MIL-F-8785C low-altitude model). At a random time between 3 and 5 s one rotor (which one is hidden) fails completely and nothing announces it; the vehicle must stay within 5 m of its setpoint, not touch the ground 3 m below, and be back within 0.5 m during the last 3 s of the 15 s episode; spinning about yaw is allowed. The textbook cascade does not survive: the geometric SE(3) law of the sanity tier and an LQR on the hover linearisation both hit the ground within about a second of the failure on 40/40 fresh seeds. The classical solution is reduced-attitude control (Mueller and D'Andrea 2014): give up yaw, let the vehicle spin and steer the spin axis; it needs the dead rotor identified within tens of milliseconds (a reaction 60 ms late costs the true-state incumbent a quarter of the seeds), an attitude estimate that survives 25 rad/s of spin with tracker frames 30 ms old, and a design that copes with the slow spin-up of this vehicle (yaw torque 0.26 N m against 0.06 kg m^2), during which the dead corner cannot be lifted. This task flies a balanced vehicle (centre of mass within +-2 mm horizontally). There is no trained RL incumbent; the incumbent is the published method run with the true state (incumbent_references/quadrotor). Gate: recovers on 0.9 of the seeds on the base scenario and on each of four perturbations (mass +-20 %, thrust -15 %, motor lag 60 ms, wind 8-11 m/s). Measured on 40 fresh seeds, the solution of quadrotor-rotor-failure recovers on 40/40 (base), 40/40 (mass), 40/40 (thrust), 3/40 (motor lag 60 ms) and 31/40 (wind 8-11 m/s): a 60 ms lag doubles the tip rate the late reaction leaves and its gains are designed for 30 ms. Solved in the effort-max sweep of 2026-11-24/25 by all six sessions (Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2); the solution/ controller is one of theirs (one cascade before and after the failure, a reduced-attitude loop about the principal axis, drag and thrust per command identified from the accelerometer): 80/80 fresh seeds on the base scenario and on each of the four perturbations.

Gate. per-seed success is the `recovers` rung of this benchmark's ladder ['airborne', 'contained', 'recovers', 'steady'] (`--success-mode recovers`), not the evaluator's default flag; the task is solved when the success rate over the grader's held-out seeds is `>= 0.9`; the same gate must also hold on the sibling scenario(s) `mass_v1`, `weak_thrust_v1`, `slow_motor_v1`, `windy_v1` (`--scenarios`)

The agent's final message

The controller at `/app/work/controller.py` passes the gate: the evaluator reports `solved=True` on seeds 0–4 with 5/5 in `nominal_v0`, `mass_v1`, `weak_thrust_v1`, `slow_motor_v1` and `windy_v1`. All four rungs are at 1.00, and the controller takes about 0.10 ms per call. I stopped at 31 of the 60 minutes because it already passes with margin. Beyond the visible seeds, I ran the final file in-process on 50 other seeds per scenario (300–349), and every episode recovered and also met the `steady` rung.
