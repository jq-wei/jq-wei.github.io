Built after the paper's sweep to test whether a task can force a mechanism outside the catalogue (docs/exploration_new_mechanisms.md, docs/soft_arm_spec.md). The -proprio tasks replace the full-state observation by on-board sensing: encoders, IMU and foot contacts on the humanoid, pressures and bend angles on the soft arm. Not part of the benchmark tiers or of the paper's main counts; the soft arm is reported in the paper as a plant outside the sweep, the other tasks are not; all sessions ran under the paper's protocol.

The task. Exploratory task (not one of the benchmark tiers reported in the paper; see docs/exploration_new_mechanisms.md): the humanoid-forward gate on uneven, tilted ground drawn per seed (2 cm bumps, 1.5-3 degrees uphill or downhill, 1-2 degrees sideways), the ground not observed.

The repository gaits pass 1/40: the forward and robust SIMBICON designs lose their balance sideways within the first metres of cross-slope. Of the 44 passing agent-written walkers of the flat tasks, screened on eight fresh seeds, one passes 8/8 (the oracle), three pass 4/8 and most fall before 3 m; what helps is placement feedback in the frontal plane, roll stabilisation through the stance hip and foot clearance. The harder sibling uneven_v2 (3 cm bumps, up to 5 degrees of pitch and 3 sideways) is passed by the same oracle at 0.925 and by nothing else known.

The agent's final message

The controller in `/app/work/controller.py` passes the `forward` rung on 130 of the 132 held-out `uneven_v1` seeds I tested (about 98.5%), against the 90% gate. The official evaluator passes all 5 visible seeds (`solved=True`): every run survives all 1000 steps, the mass centre covers about 4.5 m on average (3 m needed), and the torso never pitches more than 0.14 rad. It doesn't reach the `brisk` rung (8 m). I stopped at 18:14, about 47 of the 60 minutes used. **How it walks.** The feet are spheres, so the robot can't stand still and has to keep stepping. The controller is a hand-built walking gait (SIMBICON-style, no learning): - **Stepping:** the legs swap when the swing foot touches down after at least 0.16 s, or after 0.28 s regardless. - **Where the foot lands:** the swing foot is placed ahead of and beside the mass centre according to the body's speed, the speed error, and how far the mass centre is ahead of the standing foot. - **Upper body:** the standing leg's hip keeps the pelvis upright and pointed forward; the abdomen and arms are held stiff. **What made the difference:** 1. **Joint chatter:** large damping gains made the light joints oscillate at the control rate; small ones fixed it. 2. **Pelvis, not torso:** the hip joints act on the pelvis, and the weak abdomen lets the torso bend 0.4 rad away from it, so I control the pelvis angle instead. 3. **Stance-foot distance term:** stepping further when the mass centre is far ahead of the standing foot took 24 seeds…

Controller family: SIMBICON

A two-state SIMBICON walker places the swing foot via a velocity- and COM-offset-based Raibert rule converted to hip joint PD targets, while the stance hip PD regulates pelvis pitch/roll/yaw with a capped torque minus the swing hip torque, and the arms and abdomen are held at zero.

Controller family: Raibert / foot placement

A stance/swing state machine alternates legs at confirmed touchdowns, balancing the stance leg's pelvis lean with hip PD and a stiff knee while the swing hip is driven to a Raibert foot target (velocity-based, with lateral width) closed on measured foot position, with hip_z steering heading back to +x.
