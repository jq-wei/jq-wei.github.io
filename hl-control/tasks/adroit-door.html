The task. Dexterous manipulation with no task-space interface: the controller maps the task's own observation vector to one position-servo set point per joint (24 hand joints plus the arm's) every 10 ms, 200 steps per episode, contact-rich, on the Adroit suite of gymnasium-robotics 1.4.2.

The field's method is reinforcement learning with demonstrations (DAPG); its released policy is measured in rl_references/adroit. Middle tier. Unlatching by gripping the handle and rolling the forearm came quickly; opening the door did not: the latch has to stay turned until the bolt is clear, the hand's sideways reach ends before the door is open, and a hand that stops in the door's path blocks it. The arm slide, a soft and lightly damped servo, is not observed here and has to be inferred from the palm position before it can be damped. Our passing design (grip, roll, pull back, drag the handle along two legs, flick the wrist to let go) took about forty minutes including two rounds of the repository tuner over twelve parameters.

I stopped at about 46 of the 60 minutes with that version in place. ## How it works The controller keeps a private copy of the door model for forward kinematics and gravity torques only; it never steps it. Each call it: - **Estimates** the unobserved arm slide and the door-frame position from the palm and handle points. - **Approaches and grasps**: moves the hand over the lever, lowers it and curls the fingers round the lever. - **Unlatches**: rolls the arm with the lever about the latch axis, leading the measured latch angle. - **Pulls**: follows the handle's arc about the hinge, leading the measured hinge angle. - **Lets go**: at about 0.6 rad (earlier if the door is fast) it straightens the fingers, swings clear and retracts, and the door coasts to its stop and stays there on hinge friction.
