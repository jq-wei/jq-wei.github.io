It does not reach `crouch` or `stand` (both 0), which this task does not grade. ## How it works A small state machine sets joint-angle targets, and a soft joint PD turns them into motor commands (no model, no learning): 1. **Roll onto the back** (face-down or half-turned starts). The higher-side leg is flexed in front of the body and pressed across the midline, knee bent and then straightened, so it levers the pelvis over. The waist twists so the chest leads, and the lower arm is tucked so it doesn't prop the chest. This repeats every 1.5 s until the pelvis faces up, then the leg is laid back and gravity finishes the turn. 2. **Sit up.** Curl the abdomen and flex both hips with straight legs; the body jack-knifes and ends folded forward over the legs. 3. **Unfold and hold.** A 2 s ramp brings the trunk up to a seated posture leaning about 10° forward, with the root at 0.52 m against the 0.45 m threshold. 4. **Retry.** If the root drops below 0.35 m for 0.6 s, it starts over from the roll. The controller is finished in `/app/work/controller.py` and clears the 0.9 gate on everything I tested. It sits up on about 95% of seeds: 228 of 240 in my own simulation runs, and 79 of 80 (seeds 0–79) in the official evaluator. Time used was about 43 of the 60 minutes. **How it works** (the file's docstring explains each gain). It switches between two modes, using the torso's orientation from the root quaternion: - **Roll** (starting face down or on a side, or after falling over): a two-step joint pattern, repeated, rolls the body onto its back. It is mirrored left/right depending on which side is lower. If the chest still points down after 4 s, it tries the other direction. I found the pattern with an offline cross-entropy search on 11 visible seeds; no neural networks or gradients. - **Sit-up and hold:** hip torque is set from how far the trunk leans back. The flexion torque is capped at about 78 N·m: enough to lift the trunk, too little to lift the straight legs, so the legs stay on the floor as a counterweight. Two refinements: - A leg still raised after the roll is lowered first. - The trunk is held leaning slightly forward, so the hip extensors keep it up and press the legs into the floor. The root sits at about 0.52 m. - If the body ends up lying low on its front or side, it goes back to rolling. **What made the difference** - A sit-up straight from the lying pose only lifted the legs, because the legs and trunk have almost equal weight moments about the hips…
