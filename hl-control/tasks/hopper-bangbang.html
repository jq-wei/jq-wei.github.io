more

Each motor command is quantised to -1, 0 or +1 before it is applied, so proportional-derivative servoing has no small corrections and every joint is driven at full gear or not at all; a design must modulate (pulse-width, sliding-mode, dithering) or build the gait around the three levels. Physics and sensing nominal; gate forward (3 m) on 0.9 of the seeds. Open: the hopper-forward oracle scores 0 with bang-bang motors.

Gate. per-seed success is the `forward` rung of this benchmark's ladder ['alive', 'forward', 'brisk', 'fast'] (`--success-mode forward`), not the evaluator's default flag; the task is solved when the success rate over the grader's held-out seeds is `>= 0.9`

The expected held-out rate is therefore around 0.5, below the 0.9 gate. The failing seeds either fall in the first second after an oversized early hop (pitch leaves the band on a straight-leg landing), or stay alive but stop just short of 3 m. **What the controller does.** Every joint runs a ternary sliding-mode rule: a full 200 N m pulse fires only when a surface `error + T * error_rate` leaves a deadband sized to the measured per-pulse kick (about 1 rad/s of pitch rate per pulse). In stance the hip holds torso pitch, the knee regulates the leg lean (pitch minus thigh) and fires a timed, speed-capped extension burst for the hop, and the ankle balances the torso ahead of the foot. In flight the knee folds then re-extends for landing, but any knee pulse that would push pitch the wrong way is vetoed, and the knee takes over pitch when the thigh sits at its 0 rad limit where the hip has no authority. Knee extension pulses are cancelled before the knee can slam its stop, which was the dominant cause of pitch falls…

Controller family: sliding mode

A touchdown-triggered state machine applies ternary bang-bang sliding-surface pulses at hip, knee and ankle to hold torso pitch, regulate leg lean and balance over the foot in stance, fires a burst of knee-extension pulses for the hop, and in flight controls pitch, knee fold and foot level.
