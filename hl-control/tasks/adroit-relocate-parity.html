The task. Dexterous manipulation with no task-space interface: the controller maps the task's own observation vector to one position-servo set point per joint (24 hand joints plus the arm's) every 10 ms, 200 steps per episode, contact-rich, on the Adroit suite of gymnasium-robotics 1.4.2.

The field's method is reinforcement learning with demonstrations (DAPG); its released policy is measured in rl_references/adroit. The relocate gate plus a mean return of at least 0.9 of the DAPG policy's, measured on the grader's own seeds (4520; bound 4068). The return is dominated by the steps the ball spends within 0.05 m of the target, so the bound asks for the ball at the target about as early as the RL policy puts it there (after about 50 of 200 steps) and for keeping it there. Shorter phases cost grasp reliability: our fast script reaches 4177 on fresh seeds (first success after 62 steps, hold 120/120) and loses the ball on a low-friction plant.

The agent's final message

`/app/work/controller.py` passes both gates on every seed block I tried. On 200 fresh seeds (3000–3199) the official evaluator reports hold on 200/200 and a mean return of 4814 against the 4068 threshold; the reference policy scores 4428. First success comes at step 42.5 on average, against 49 for the reference. The exact command from the task on seeds 0–4 gives 5/5 and 4801. The controller is a three-phase state machine with no learning: - **Approach:** a private copy of the plant model gives the palm position and Jacobian, and a bounded least-squares IK turns the desired palm position into arm joint targets. The palm only descends once it is above the ball. - **Close:** the fingers start closing during the last ~2 cm of the descent, with the thumb swinging under the ball. - **Carry:** the palm reference is the target plus the measured palm–ball offset, so the ball itself is servoed onto the target. The wrist is flexed and the palm arched to keep the ball in the finger hook. The arm servo cancels each joint's own spring and adds gravity compensation plus PD. The docstring explains each gain.
