Exploration task. Built after the paper's sweep to test whether a task can force a mechanism outside the catalogue (docs/exploration_new_mechanisms.md, docs/soft_arm_spec.md). The -proprio tasks replace the full-state observation by on-board sensing: encoders, IMU and foot contacts on the humanoid, pressures and bend angles on the soft arm. Not part of the benchmark tiers or of the paper's main counts; the soft arm is reported in the paper as a plant outside the sweep, the other tasks are not; all sessions ran under the paper's protocol.

The task. Exploratory task (not one of the benchmark tiers reported in the paper; see docs/exploration_new_mechanisms.md): the humanoid-brisk gate (8 m in 15 s, 0.53 m/s, alive) on the same physics with proprioceptive sensing only, i.e.

more

joint encoders, a torso IMU (gyro, gravity direction, specific force with an unknown constant bias per seed) and foot contact switches. Root height, horizontal velocity, heading, the mass centre and the foot positions have to be reconstructed, and at this speed the heading is the binding estimate: the full-observation brisk design falls behind any estimator because it servos the heading stiffly to a value no proprioceptive walker can know; the oracle slows the gait and steers through the foot placement instead. Flat counterpart of humanoid-forward-proprio one rung up.

Gate. per-seed success is the `brisk` rung of this benchmark's ladder ['alive', 'forward', 'brisk', 'fast'] (`--success-mode brisk`), not the evaluator's default flag; the task is solved when the success rate over the grader's held-out seeds is `>= 0.9`

Each step it estimates the full humanoid state from onboard sensors, samples 128 noisy piecewise-linear control plans, rolls them out in a internal MuJoCo model, picks the lowest-cost plan under a speed/upright/capture-point/feet-gap cost, and applies its first command.
